# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, two surfaces.

The homeowner is the primary audience of the public Home Value Site (HVS). They are a US homeowner on a phone or laptop, arriving from a brokerage's postcard, ad, email, or website, curious about one thing: what their home is worth right now. They are not logged in, they have not asked for a sales call, and they will leave in under a minute if the page asks for anything before it gives them a number. The job to be done is "tell me my number, then show me why I should believe it".

The brokerage operator is the audience of the console. They are a marketing director, broker-owner, or growth lead at a real estate brokerage that licenses HVS from Reliance. They set up the site, put it on their domain or embed it in their existing website, brand it, and then watch it as a line of business: how many homeowners came, how many left contact details, which areas are hot, which agents follow up. They live in this console a few times a week alongside their CRM.

Reliance itself is a stakeholder, not a user: the platform must look like a Reliance product while disappearing behind each brokerage's brand on the public pages.

## Product Purpose

Reliance HVS is a rebuild of Percy's home valuation product as a white-label platform. A homeowner enters an address, and a single report answers the questions they actually have: what the home is worth, what the range and confidence are, how much equity they likely hold, how many active buyers are looking for a home like theirs, and how the neighborhood market is moving. Every answer is designed to earn the homeowner's contact details without ever gating the number behind a form.

Success for the homeowner is a report that feels like a gift, not a trap. Success for the brokerage is a measurable lead pipeline with clear attribution and a setup that a marketing director can complete without an engineer. Success for Reliance is a product that scales across hundreds of brokerages through configuration, not custom code.

## Positioning

The home valuation site that gives homeowners the whole picture instantly, and gives brokerages a line of business they can actually see.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA on the public site: enter an address and get the report. Secondary CTA inside the report: talk to a local agent, or unlock buyer matches with an email.
- The line a homeowner remembers after 10 seconds: "I got my number in seconds, and there are buyers looking for a home like mine."
- Belief ladder for the homeowner: this will be instant and free, the number is credible because it shows its sources and range, there is more here than a number (equity, buyers, market), and the brokerage behind it is local and trustworthy.
- Proof on hand: none supplied yet. The mockup uses a fictional brokerage and fictional but realistic data. Real customer logos, MLS disclaimers, and testimonials will come from Reliance during the SOW.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, generous. The public pages should feel like an Apple product page for a home: one idea per screen, large confident typography, real photography, and numbers that are presented with the care of a bank statement. The tone is direct and warm, never salesy. Copy says the number first and explains second. The console is quieter still: it should feel like a well-made operations tool that a non-technical marketer trusts on first use.

## Anti-references

- The current Percy HVS pages (V1, V2, Hawaii): a full-width house photo with a search bar dropped on top, then a long scroll of boxed widgets with mismatched styling. Do not repeat the "search bar on a stock photo" hero.
- Zillow and Redfin: dense listing-portal UI with competing modules, ads, and blue links everywhere.
- Generic real estate templates: navy and gold, script fonts, "Find your dream home" copy.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: gradient stat cards, purple accents, decorative charts with no question behind them.

## Design Principles

- Give the number first. Nothing about the homeowner's value, range, or equity is ever hidden behind a form. Lead capture is earned by depth, not imposed by a gate.
- One idea per screen. Each section of the report answers one homeowner question with one dominant figure or visual, then supporting detail.
- Show the sources. Every estimate is accompanied by its range, its confidence, and where it came from. Credibility is a design feature.
- The brand is the brokerage's. The platform's design language is a neutral, elegant frame; a brokerage's color, logo, footer, and voice slot into it without custom code. Customization is composable, not free-form.
- Analytics are a product, not a byproduct. Every interaction on the public pages is designed to produce a meaningful signal the console can show as a line-of-business metric.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA on both surfaces. Body text at or above 4.5:1 contrast, visible focus states, full keyboard paths for the address search and all forms, reduced-motion alternatives for every animation, and 44px touch targets on mobile. Charts never rely on color alone and always have a text or table equivalent. The public site is read by people of every age and technical comfort level, so copy stays plain and numbers stay large.
